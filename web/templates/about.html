Source

Every number except bWAR, ERA+ and the Baseball Savant stats comes from FanGraphs' leaderboards: one line per player per season (all players, not only qualified ones), a line per club for traded players, and FanGraphs' own team totals. During the season the current year uses FanGraphs' live split, which includes the day's games, and refreshes every hour. Finished seasons are refetched once a month to pick up FanGraphs' recalculations (WAR, park factors, wOBA constants).

FanGraphs counts the National Association (1871 to 1875), the American Association, Union Association, Players' League and Federal League, and the Negro Leagues (1920 to 1948) as major leagues, so those seasons are here too.

fWAR and bWAR

fWAR is FanGraphs' wins above replacement, the WAR in every FanGraphs column on this site (shown as fWAR; links still use WAR). bWAR is Baseball-Reference's, from its daily WAR files, refreshed once a day. Both use the same framework and the same replacement level, 1,000 WAR across a full season. They differ in method, most of all for pitchers: fWAR credits a pitcher for his FIP (strikeouts, walks, hit batters and home runs, with average results on balls in play), while bWAR starts from the runs he actually allowed and adjusts for the defense behind him. The fielding systems differ too, and fWAR includes catcher framing, so the two can disagree by several wins for one season: 1880s workhorse pitchers and framing catchers show the biggest gaps. FanGraphs' own runs-allowed version is RA9-Wins (pitching Value). Team fWAR is FanGraphs' team total; bWAR is not summed for teams.

bWAR is matched to FanGraphs players by MLB player id, or for Negro Leagues players (who have none) by name, season and club. A traded player's bWAR for each club is Baseball-Reference's figure for that stint.

Baseball Savant

Sweet-Spot%, xwOBAcon, sprint speed, pitchers' xBA, xSLG and xwOBA, and the Savant discipline table (Zone%, Z-Swing%, Z-Contact%, Chase%, Chase contact, Edge%, Swing%, Whiff%, Meatball% and Meatball swing%) come from Baseball Savant's season leaderboards, 2015 on, matched by MLB player id. They use Statcast's strike zone, so Savant's Zone% and Chase% differ from FanGraphs' Zone% and O-Swing% (Baseball Info Solutions' zone) in the Plate discipline table. Spans and careers are rebuilt from Savant's counts (pitches in and out of the zone, swings, whiffs, batted balls), so they match Savant's own career rows. The current season refreshes daily; every season once a month.

League averages

Tables show a league-average row for rates, the way Savant shows an MLB row: on the leaderboards for the seasons on show, and on player pages for the seasons he played (tick "League average under every season" for one per season). Savant's own MLB row is the league over 2015 to now; here each comparison uses the same seasons. The league is the AL and NL together ("MLB"), or the player's own league for the Negro Leagues and the 19th-century majors, added up from FanGraphs' team totals, so a rate is the league's own (all its strikeouts over all its plate appearances), not an average of players. Index stats (wRC+, ERA+, ERA-, ...) are 100 by definition.

Qualified

MLB ranks rate stats only among players with 3.1 plate appearances (hitters) or 1 inning pitched (pitchers) per game their team plays: 502 PA and 162 IP over a 162-game season. That is what Qualified means here, for every rate (AVG, OBP, wRC+, K%, ERA, FIP, and so on). Counting stats (home runs, strikeouts, fWAR, bWAR) have no minimum, so the leader boards for those include everyone. Over several seasons the site uses FanGraphs' multi-season cut: 1,000 PA or 300 IP, or the sum of the single-season minimums if that is lower.

Seasons and careers

Over a span of seasons, counting stats add up. AVG, OBP, SLG, OPS, ISO, BABIP, K% and BB% for hitters, and ERA, WHIP, K/9, BB/9, HR/9, K%, BB% and K-BB% for pitchers, are rebuilt from the summed parts. Rates that need league constants (wOBA, wRC+, FIP, xFIP, SIERA) are averaged, weighted by plate appearances or innings; plate-discipline and pitch rates by pitches; Statcast rates by batted balls.

Era-adjusted charts

On the chart, "Era-adjusted" replaces each value with its percentile that season (100 = best), so a 1968 OBP is measured against 1968 pitching. A player is ranked against that season's qualified players in his league group, and a club against every club in its group: the AL and NL together, and each other league (National Association, American Association, Union Association, Players' League, Federal League, each Negro League) on its own, because those leagues played shorter schedules against their own competition. A traded player counts in the group where he played most. Filters for team, league or position only hide points; they never change who a value is ranked against. Over a span, a point is the average of its season percentiles weighted by plate appearances (innings for pitchers). Lower-is-better stats (ERA, K% for hitters) are turned round, so 100 is always best.
